# My Water Rocket

**Program:** NC State MSEN Summer Scholars — STEM on the Move  
**When:** July 6–10, 2026  
**Status:** Built and launched during camp

## What I was trying to do

The challenge was to build a stable water rocket that could fly as high as possible. I had to think about the fins, balance, and weight as well as how to put the rocket together.

## What I built

I used a plastic bottle, cork, water, foam-board fins, a hand pump, and balancing weight. I added fins to help the rocket stay stable and placed weight near the top to help with balance.

The launch helped me understand thrust: air pressure pushed water down, and the rocket moved up. The design also gave me a way to think about gravity, air resistance, and stability using something I could actually test.

## What happened

My camp journal records the final launch height as **77 feet**. I have not included a measurement method or a series of trial readings because those are not recorded in my saved notes. The result is from that launch, not an average across several tests.

## What I would change next

I would test one variable at a time, such as water volume, fin shape, or nose weight. For every launch, I would record the setup and height using the same measurement method. That would help me work out whether a change really helped.

I enjoyed this project because I could connect what I was learning in physics to a rocket I built. It also showed me why a good test record matters when I want to improve a design.
