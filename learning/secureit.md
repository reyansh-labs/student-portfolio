# Learning About Cybersecurity

I'm taking part in NC State's **2026–27 SecureIT Cybersecurity Workshops**. I have attended two sessions so far, on **September 12** and **October 10, 2026**.

I'm interested in cybersecurity because I want to understand how people protect the systems they build. Making a program work is one part of the job; thinking about how it could fail or be misused is another.

## September 12: Ciphers, phishing, and AI patterns

At my first workshop, I solved Caesar, Pigpen, and number-to-letter puzzles. I also practiced sorting real and scam emails and checking clues such as sender domains, link destinations, and pressure to act quickly.

I tried Quick, Draw! and worked with a webcam model in Teachable Machine. I trained it to recognize poses, tried to fool it with a similar pose, and retrained it with better examples. This helped me see how the examples used to train a model affect its results.

I learned about confidentiality, integrity, and availability: keeping information private, protecting it from unwanted changes, and making sure it can be accessed when needed. Another useful lesson was that good writing does not prove an email is safe.

## October 10: Thinking like an attacker and a defender

At my second workshop, I used games and practice scenarios to think about what an opponent might do next. The activities included Ultimate Tic-Tac-Toe, a Red Team and Blue Team data-breach exercise, and testing rules for a simple phishing detector.

I also considered when to trust, check, or reject an AI recommendation. A confident answer can still be wrong. Adding stricter detection rules can catch more suspicious messages, but it can also flag safe ones.

I worked with a partner on a phishing-awareness poster called **The Coral Reef**. We listed warning signs and created a pretend payment-request email for the classroom activity. I practiced looking at the sender, links, and request together instead of deciding from just one clue.
